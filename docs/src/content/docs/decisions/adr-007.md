**Date**: 2026-09-29
**Status**: Decided

## Context

The renderer keeps its live state under a key derived from the dataset's id and version, exposes no channel, and may not be modified. Left alone, a rehearsal and the session that follows it over the same revision share one slot, and two live mounts of one revision cross-write. Landing on a timer would delete outcomes recorded since the last tick on a refresh.

## Decision

The console mounts the artifact from a served same-origin URL, never a `data:` URL, with a sandbox that keeps the origin. Before the iframe boots it seeds the slot from the run's server-side capture exactly once; it lands every subsequent renderer write through the capture port on the `storage` event the same-origin iframe's write fires at the console window, with a low-frequency interval and a landing at the close as a safety net; it takes a tab-scoped ownership token, kept in session storage and renewed by a heartbeat in a leased local-storage entry, so a refresh re-enters as the same owner and a second tab is refused with an explicit message while a crashed tab frees the slot after one lease; and it clears the slot when the run is left. The key derivation is asserted against the pinned template at the renderer's hash.

## Rationale

- Event-driven landing removes the unlanded window rather than trying to detect it, which the shared key makes impossible.
- Refusal beats merging: the slot is one area per origin, and two writers would corrupt each other whatever the cadence.
- Pinning the coupling to the renderer's key rule makes a renderer move fail the build instead of capturing nothing.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **A channel added to the renderer**: forbidden; the renderer is consumed, never forked.
- **Re-implementing the six surfaces natively**: contradicts the frozen architecture and the real-artifact embed the design locked.
- **Landing on an interval**: breaks refresh survival, a critical claim.
